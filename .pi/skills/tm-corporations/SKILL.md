# Corporations

Format: 2-player, base game + Prelude, Corporate Era, Draft variant.

Refer to `tm-strategy` for the terraformer vs. engine archetype axis —
each corporation naturally leans toward one end.

---

## Credicor (CORP01)

Starts with 57 MC. Effect: you get 4 MC back when playing a card or standard project costing 20+ MC.

Credicor is a good rush corp. It's rich starting capital and ability to standard project greeneries for effectively 19 MC means it can fight on the ground and for milestones like Gardener and awards like Landlord even without much plant production. Its discount on expensive space events can also come in handy when rushing.

A very powerful synergy is "Creditech" (Credicor + Standard Technology). With this combination, standard greeneries only cost 16 MC and standard cities only 18 MC, becoming quite efficient.

However, the rich starting capital also means Credicor can effectively play engine setups. It's a flexible corporation with a high floor, meaning it's rarely bad.

## Ecoline (CORP02)

Starts with 36 MC, 2 plant production, 3 plants. Tags: Plant. Effect: you may convert 7 plants (instead of 8) into greeneries.

Ecoline is the quintessential rush corp with its plant start and discount on plant conversions.

Since Ecoline is so dominant on the ground, taking up a lot of the scoring on the oxygen track, it wants ways to increase the other parameters. It loves space events that terraform and wants to have early heat production to make sure the game finishes as quickly as possible.

Cards like Soletta, Mohole Area and Black Polar Dust become much better in the hands of Ecoline.

On the Tharsis map, Ecoline typically goes for the Gardener and Terraformer milestones and tries to fund the Landlord award at 8 MC if it gets off to a dominant lead on that with strong plant production.

## Helion (CORP03)

Starts with 42 MC, 3 heat production. Tags: Space. Effect: you may use heat as MC (1:1).

Helion is a relatively weak corporation for the most part.

It thrives best in rush setups since it has built-in heat production to push the temperature track. However, the ability to use heat as MC means it flexible and can also go in an engine direction.

Heat production cards become more valuable.

Temperature-locked cards like Trees and Fish become slightly stronger in the hands of Helion. Oxygen-locked cards like Predators and Birds become slightly weaker.

## Mining Guild (CORP04)

Starts with 30 MC, 5 steel, 1 steel production. Tags: 2x Building,
Building. Effect: each time you place a tile on get a steel and/or titanium bonus, increase steel production 1 step.

Mining Guild (MG) is an engine corp that needs early tiles to boost its steel production.

Tiles should generally go exclusively on mineral spots in the early game. Once it's up to 4-5 steel prod, MG can start to prioritize other placements as well.

Since MG wants to play a lot of building cards with steel, it also loves energy production, since many of those steel cards require reducing your energy prod.

MG loves card draw. Without it, it can have a hard time finding enough cards with building tags to spend all its accumulated steel on.

MG often gets an easy Builder milestone on Tharsis because of the building tags it plays and the two building tags on the corp itself. It can also frequently fund the Miner award at 8 MC because of the escalating steel production it gets by placing tiles.

## Interplanetary Cinematics (CORP05)

Starts with 30 MC, 20 steel. Tags: Building. Effect: each time you play an event, gain 2 MC.

Interplanetary Cinematics (IC) is a strong corporation with very good starting capital and a strong effect.

The only drawback of IC is that most of the starting capital is steel, which can sometimes be hard to spend. If IC can spend its steel early, it usually gets off to a very strong start. Without steel targets, it can be pretty bad, although IC can sometimes be playable even without steel targets, since they are quite common in base + prelude and likely to turn up in drafts.

Like Mining Guild, IC wants energy production to be able to play building cards that consume energy prod.

Advanced Alloys is very potent in the starting hand for IC.

Media Group and Optimal Aerobraking are inherently strong cards that stack well with the IC effect.

IC has an advantage on the Builder milestone.

## Inventrix (CORP06)

Starts with 45 MC. Tags: Science. Effect: your global requirements are +/- 2 steps. As first action, draw 3 cards.

Inventrix is a relatively weak but flexible corporation. It draws three cards and has a science tag, both of which can give a flying start to engine setups with science payoffs. But it can also play more to the ground with terraforming, since its effect allows it to play plant production cards earlier than other corps.

The main problem for Inventrix is that it often ends up being poor and not being able to afford all its cards. Inventrix loves early economic development like Business Empire (prelude) or basic production cards like Sponsors and Acquired Company.

Inventrix can be positioned to get an early Planner milestone on Tharsis, but it's often a trap, since it pays so much for buying enough cards and claiming the milestone that it can severely slow down its development. This is true for most corps, but especially for Inventrix who is generally poor to begin with.

## Phobolog (CORP07)

Starts with 23 MC, 10 titanium. Tags: Space. Effect: your titanium is worth 1 MC extra (4 MC each instead of 3).

Phobolog is one of the weakest corporations, but it can be situationally strong.

The problem with Phobolog is that most of its economy is bound to titanium, and there are a limited number of early development cards with space tags in base + prelude.

A Jovian strategy, ideally starting with gen 1 Io Mining Industries or Asteroid Mining, can be quite strong with Phobolog. Occasionally, Phobolog can also rush by starting with cards like Soletta, Giant Ice Asteroid or Deimos Down.

Phobolog likes titanium production since it's alloyed by default.

---

## Tharsis Republic (CORP08)

Starts with 40 MC. Tags: Building. Effect: when any city tile is placed on Mars, increase MC production 1 step. When you place a city, gain 3 MC.

Tharsis Republic is a decent corporation on the Tharsis map and especially on the Elysium map, but relatively weak on Hellas where the ground game is less important.

On Elysium, it can dominate the central ground game surrounding the oceans and lock up the Estate Dealer award.

On its namesake map Tharsis, it has an obvious advantage on the Mayor milestone, but also a decent advantage on the Banker award.

Because Tharsis Republic tends to get many cities, it likes to get plant production that will produce greeneries that score adjacency points for those cities.

---

## Thorgate (CORP09)

Starts with 48 MC, 1 energy production. Tags: Power. Effect: power cards and Power Plant standard project cost 3 MC less.

Thorgate is one of the weakest corporations in the game. The starting capital is okay, but the discount on power tags isn't really all that great, since you're rarely playing many of those.

It has a higher floor than other weak corps like UNMI and Phobolog, but is rarely ever spectacular.

It likes to play cards that require energy production.

---

## United Nations Mars Initiative (CORP10)

Starts with 40 MC. Tags: Earth. Effect: after you increase your TR this generation, you may pay 3 MC to increase TR 1 additional step.

United Nations Mars Initiative (UNMI) is the weakest corp in base + prelude. To be playable, it needs consistent terraforming, i.e. a way to get TR every gen so it can use its effect. If not, it's very weak.

UNMI likes cards that gives consistent terraforming, such as oxygen bumpers (Steelworks/Ore Processor/Ironworks) when it has enough energy production for them, microbe bumpers like GHG Producing Bacteria or Regolith Eaters when combined with Extreme Cold Fungus, space events and big heat production cards like Soletta.

If UNMI can get consistent terraforming every generation, it eventually becomes rich enough that it can pivot into engine strategies with science card draw, or simply finish out the game while it's still in the lead.

UNMI typically gets the Terraformer milestone by default and the Benefactor award on Elysium. It struggles a lot on milestones and awards on Hellas.

## Teractor (CORP11)

Starts with 60 MC. Tags: Earth. Effect: Earth-tagged cards cost 3 MC less.

Teractor has the richest starting MC of any corp, but no real built-in strategic direction. Therefore, it's flexible.

Teractor excels at making expensive plays early, like Io Mining Industries and other expensive development plays.

If the starting hand has earth tags like Acquired Company, Cartel, Nuclear Zone and others, Teractor becomes a lot stronger.

---

## Saturn Systems (CORP12)

Starts with 42 MC, 1 titanium production. Tags: Jovian. Effect: each time any Jovian tag is played (including this), increase MC production 1 step.

Saturn Systems has a built-in advantage when playing Jovian cards, which means it can play this strategy better than any other corp. However, this also means it can be an efficient rush corporation at times, since opponents will tend to hate-draft Jovian cards over other strong cards they might not normally pass in the draft.

Saturn is especially strong on the Hellas map, where it has an obvious advantage on the Rim Settler milestone.

---

## Cheung Shing Mars (CORP18)

Starts with 44 MC, 3 MC production. Tags: Building. Effect: Cards with building tags cost 2 MC less.

Cheung Shing Mars (CSM) is a strong corporation with a built-in discount on cards with building tags, which are very common in the base + prelude set. Combined with the built-in MC production, that usually guarantees a strong economic start, making CSM a good engine corporation.

CSM has a built-in advantage on the Builder milestone.

---

## Point Luna (CORP19)

Starts with 38 MC, 1 titanium production. Tags: Space, Earth. Effect: when you play an Earth tag (including this), draw 1 card.

Point Luna is the strongest corporation in the game because of its built-in card-drawing effect. When combined with early earth tags like the preludes Business Empire, UNMI Contractor and Allied Banks, or project cards like Earth Office, Acquired Company and Cartel, Point Luna can become ridiculously strong.

Point Luna is an engine corporation. It usually favors a longer game, where it can benefit from its card draw.

It thrives on all maps, but is especially potent on the "engine map" Hellas.

---

## Robinson Industries (CORP20)

Starts with 47 MC. Effect: action — spend 4 MC to increase your lowest production 1 step.

Robinson Industries is an egnine corporation that starts slow, but can build up a powerful production line with its effect.

Robinson should try to use its action to boost the most valuable production resources, primarily titanium first, followed by steel and plant (and energy when it needs to). Since MC and heat production are the weakest production types, Robinson generally wants to avoid having to use its ability on those. Therefore, it benefits greatly from early MC production, with preludes like Business Empire and Allied Banks or project cards like Acquired Company.

Robinson likes Advanced Alloys, since it usually gets a lot of mineral production. It wants to get card draw to find targets for all its minerals.

Robinson has a built-in advantage on the Generalist milestone on Elysium. It's also a constant threat on the Miner award on Tharsis.

---

## Valley Trust (CORP21)

Starts with 37 MC. Tags: Earth. Effect: As first action, draw 3 Prelude cards and play 1 of them. When you play a card with a science tag, you pay 2 MC less.

Valley Trust is a strong corporation that gets an explosive start with 3 preludes. This means it can effectively rush, but it is also a flexible corp that can go for engine setups. The discount on science tags is very helpful in engine setups.

Its flexibility makes it a solid performer on all maps and in most circumstances.

---

## Vitor (CORP22)

Starts with 45 MC. Tags: Earth. Effect: when you play a card with a non-negative VP icon, gain 3 MC. As first action, fund an award for free.

Vitor is the quintessential engine corp. It starts relatively slowly, but can snowball into insane engine setups. Its discount on non-negative VP cards affects around 40% of the deck in base + prelude. This means that Vitor generally wants to score most of its point with VP on cards in the later game and use the early stages of the game to build up an economy and synergies. It likes longer games and often wants to hold back the temperature track (or the lagging parameter).

Vitor loves to have card draw.

The free award fund is a strength for Vitor but can also be a liability. You would preferrably like to have a clear direction with your fund when choosing Vitor, since you need to do it at the very start of the game.

Vitor is particularly strong on Hellas.
